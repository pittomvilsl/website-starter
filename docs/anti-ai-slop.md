# Anti-AI-Slop Design Standard

## Purpose

AI may accelerate production, but it must not determine the site's taste by default. Every interface must show deliberate, brand-specific human art direction.

## Default blacklist

Do not introduce these patterns automatically:

- purple-to-blue gradients
- gradient hero text
- decorative badges above every headline
- three identical icon cards in a row
- emojis in headings
- Inter everywhere by default
- Space Grotesk + Instrument Serif merely because it is trendy
- untouched shadcn components
- glassmorphism without a functional reason
- colored left-border cards as filler
- generic bento grids
- low-contrast dark mode
- Lucide icons as decoration
- universal fade-in-on-scroll
- cursor-following beams
- glow-heavy buttons/backgrounds
- random grain over gradients
- pills everywhere
- excessive rounded cards
- generic serif-italic accent words
- hover states that merely reduce opacity
- buzzword-heavy AI copy
- em-dash-heavy prose
- floating blobs, orbs, and abstract background shapes
- every block centered, with no alignment variation down the page
- SaaS/startup layout conventions applied to a business they do not fit

These are not absolute bans. They require a clear brand or usability reason.

## Composition rules

Design from content and hierarchy, not from cards.

Before using a card grid, consider:
- typography
- whitespace
- editorial layout
- split composition
- comparison tables
- timelines
- diagrams
- photography
- asymmetrical grids
- numbered sections
- full-width statements

Every section must answer:
1. Why does this section exist?
2. What question does it answer?
3. What should the visitor understand or do next?

Delete filler sections.

## Component rules

- Libraries are primitives, not visual identity.
- Branded components must have intentional typography, spacing, states, and proportions.
- Icons are for comprehension, not empty decoration.
- Animations must guide attention, explain state, provide feedback, or improve storytelling.
- Mobile is designed independently where necessary.
- Spacing must follow the project scale; avoid arbitrary one-off values without reason.

## Copy rules

Prefer concrete language over vague buzzwords like seamless, effortless, unlock, empower, elevate, reimagined, cutting-edge, next-generation, or transform your experience.

Avoid formulaic AI constructions such as:
- "not just X, but Y"
- repetitive rhetorical questions
- three-part marketing lists in every paragraph
- unnecessary em dashes
- slogans that could fit any company

## Brand-transfer test

Temporarily imagine the logo, company name, and brand colors are removed.

If the section could be moved unchanged to a crypto, AI, recruitment, or SaaS website, it is probably too generic and should be redesigned.

## Signature-element rule

Each substantial site or landing page should have at least one memorable brand-specific design idea.

One strong idea is better than ten gimmicks.

## Final audit

- [ ] Clear project-specific art direction is visible.
- [ ] Typography feels intentional rather than default.
- [ ] Spacing is consistent.
- [ ] Repeated cards are justified.
- [ ] Component-library defaults are visibly customized.
- [ ] Icons are functional and not excessive.
- [ ] No generic gradient/glow/glass treatment was added without reason.
- [ ] Motion is purposeful.
- [ ] Mobile composition is deliberate.
- [ ] Copy is concrete and human.
- [ ] The page passes the brand-transfer test.
- [ ] At least one brand-specific visual idea is present where appropriate.

A page is not finished because it looks modern. It is finished when it feels coherent, credible, useful, and unmistakably made for this brand.
